[engaged storyteller] That country was Thailand. In 2021, Thailand supplied almost 100% of the fresh durians China imported.

Then, in 2022, China opened the door to Vietnam.

What happened next was a gold rush.

Vietnam's durian exports were worth 180 million dollars in 2021. This year, they're expected to hit about 4 billion. The land planted with durian grew more than fivefold in a decade, to around 200,000 hectares.

Coffee farmers cut down coffee trees. In southern Thailand, farmers ripped out rubber trees to make room.

In Malaysia, so many growers switched that mangosteen, once the cheap fruit, now costs more than some premium durians.

By 2025, Vietnam had overtaken Thailand as China's biggest supplier by volume. Thailand still earned more: just under 4 billion dollars, against Vietnam's 3.44 billion.

But every gold rush has casualties.

A farmer named Hung switched his whole farm from coffee to durian. Two years later, he couldn't meet the quality standards buyers demanded. He went back to coffee, and told AFP he would never try durian again.

Even Lan, the farmer who got rich, still grows coffee. He compares growing durian to raising a child: you can do everything right and still fail.
