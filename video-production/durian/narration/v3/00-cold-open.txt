[engaged storyteller] In the highlands of Vietnam, there's a farmer named Pham Xuan Lan.

This land runs on coffee. Vietnam is the second-biggest coffee producer on Earth.

But Lan has 400 other trees now. This year, he expects them to earn him about 76,000 dollars. If he'd stuck with coffee alone? Roughly 10,000.

Those trees paid for a car. Houses for his children. A villa.

"You can live okay with coffee," he told AFP, "but it's durian that makes you rich."

And almost all of that money comes from one place.

China buys around 90% of all the durians the world exports. Last year it spent almost 7.5 billion dollars on them. That's nearly twelve times what it spent a decade earlier.

Across Southeast Asia, farmers have replanted around one customer's appetite.

And this year, the price is falling.

So what happens when an entire export industry depends on one buyer?
