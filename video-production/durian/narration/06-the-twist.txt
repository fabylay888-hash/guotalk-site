And there's one more move on the board.

China is growing its own.

On the island of Hainan, there are now more than 3,000 hectares of durian orchards. Growers there sell their fruit as tree-ripened, a direct shot at imports picked early for the journey.

So should Southeast Asia panic?

Not yet. In 2025, China's homegrown durians amounted to less than 1% of its imports, and they still cost more to produce. And a durian expert at Hainan's own agricultural academy told AFP that Chinese durians will only ever be a supplement to Southeast Asian ones.

The real risk was never Hainan. It's the math.
